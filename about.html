Our story

Why MomoSomo exists

MomoSomo didn't start with a business plan. It started with the fact that good momos were hard to find nearby, and the ones we did find were either too soft, too sweet in the chutney, or clearly reheated.

So the idea was small on purpose: pick one dish, make it properly, and keep the menu short enough that nothing on it is an afterthought. Momos, a handful of ways to have them, chutneys worth talking about, and something hot to drink alongside.

"If we can't make it fresh that day, it doesn't go on the menu."

That's the whole philosophy, really. The dough is rolled and filled every morning, not frozen and thawed. The chutney is blended in small batches so it doesn't sit around losing its edge. If a filling runs out in the evening, it runs out — we'd rather say no than serve something that isn't right.

What's different

A few things we're particular about

We steam in small batches through the day instead of one big batch in the morning, so what reaches your table has usually been out of the steamer for a few minutes, not a few hours.

The red chutney is the one thing on the menu that hasn't changed since day one — dried red chilli, garlic, and just enough sesame to round it off. Everything else, we've tweaked more than once.

We're not trying to reinvent the momo. Tandoori, Afghani, kurkure — these exist because people asked for them, not because a menu consultant suggested "fusion" items. If something doesn't sell, it comes off the menu.

What to expect

Come hungry, leave without a queue story

It's a counter-service kind of place — order, grab a table if one's free, and we'll bring it over once it's ready. Evenings can get busy, so if you're short on time, calling ahead or ordering on WhatsApp helps.

No loyalty app, no QR-code-only menu, no forced service charge debates. Just food, a fair bill, and chai if you want to sit a while longer.
